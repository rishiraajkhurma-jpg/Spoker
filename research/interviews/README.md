## What each one said

**Gulshan**
- Last sponsor came from meeting them in person at an event at IIT Bombay, then being passed to the right person. Onboarding took about a month.
- Emailed about 500 companies: 60 to 70 replied, 10 or 11 paid.
- Biggest time sink: 25-minute pitch calls with companies that then say they have no budget and offer barter.
- Lost a case-competition sponsor on timing: it clashed with placement season.
- Approves every email and checks the numbers in it.
- Would give the software a floor set a little above the real minimum, so it never sells too cheap. Would give software about 20% of what it brings in.
- Below the floor: depends on time and whether the slot is still empty.
- "Call me" or "WhatsApp me, don't email" is tracked by hand today.

**Ashutosh**
- Finds companies that sponsored other fests (for example Antaragni, IIT Kanpur). Emails from Apollo, plus LinkedIn by hand. About 20 people at one company produced 2 or 3 replies and the deal.
- About 1,000 companies, 10 to 15 people each: around 150 replied, around 100 conversations started, most of them weak.
- Most frustrating: building the database of the right people (marketing and partnership heads; Mumbai first at large companies).
- Lost sponsors by replying late to their emails.
- Team reviews emails, sometimes checks them with an AI tool.
- Wants software to find and rank companies per event (RoboWars, concerts) and give the people database too.
- Would pay about ₹5,000, or ₹10,000 to ₹15,000 per 200 companies approached on his behalf.
- Must stay human: replies, scheduling and negotiation, especially how much to give and the final amount.
- Below the floor: hold the offer and look for sponsors at the full price. A yes on a call or WhatsApp is confirmed with a signed MOU.

**Sarthak Saran**
- Sectors are split among managers (sports apparel, electronics); sponsors become category partners (hydration, electronics, apparel).
- People from Apollo or LinkedIn, then email everyone and follow up by phone. Emailed more than 30 to 50 companies.
- Closed FastenUp as electrolyte partner of the half marathon on barter after a teammate's intro.
- Most frustrating: finding emails and mailing them.
- Lost Pluckk after a long engagement that ended in a no.
- Email templates are approved by the OCs.
- Wants software that finds the right people by designation, extracts their emails and mails them; calling stays with the team.
- Would pay ₹1,000 a month for a database he can trust.
- Common reply: "I forwarded it to the right person."

**Eklavya**
- Used an Apollo database of food and beverage companies, emailed and called.
- About 80 first touches, 7 or 8 paid, reply rate around 30 to 40%.
- Most frustrating: building the sponsor database.
- Lost a sponsor because he quoted far more than the organisation had charged them before, which he did not know.
- His manager approves emails: checks the data points and that the MOU requirements are covered.
- Keep the conversation and money handling human; give contact finding to software.
- Earns barter and stall fees (a local F&B brand took a 12 by 8 ft stall).
- Below the floor: hold, and accept only if the database runs out before the minimum number of sponsors.
- Keeps a shared team database so nobody contacts a sponsor twice.

## How SponsorDesk answers this

| Pain | In the product |
|---|---|
| Building the database of the right people | Decision Makers: primary, alternative and fallback contact per company, verified email and business phone, evidence, relevance score |
| Wrong or guessed emails | Emails only from cited public pages or Crustdata; each one checked and labelled verified, corroborated, unverified, not available or outdated |
| Numbers in emails must be right | Every email waits for a named approver; the floor is never shown to sponsors |
| Offers below the minimum | Below-floor offers become a decision for a person, with a trade-value counter drafted |
| Slow replies lose sponsors | Gmail sweep classifies replies and drafts the answer for approval |
| Contacting someone twice, or after they said stop | One thread per company, a reply stops cold mail for the whole company, opt-outs are permanent |
| Not knowing a sponsor's history | Sponsors page and deal room timeline keep every email, reply, meeting and deal |
